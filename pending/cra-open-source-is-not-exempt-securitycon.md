## Session Title

Open Source Is Not Exempt: What the CRA Really Asks of Open Source Projects and Vendors

## Description

CRA is the first EU-wide law to put binding security requirements on products sold in EU. it decides which products may be placed on EU market, and its reach goes beyond EU. This matters to everyone in the ecosystem: users, contributors, project owners, and the enterprises.

In this session we will explain what the CRA is and the positions an open source project can be in, with a simple test to tell them apart. We will cover what an OSS is expected to do to comply, what vendors building on open source will be required to do, and what they will start asking project maintainers for. We will also talk about where security is heading as laws like the CRA come into force. We maintain OSS ourselves and at work and help companies comply with CRA, and from that, we will share a practical checklist drawn from applying all 71 articles of CRA to real projects.

This session will be a complete map of what CRA means for OSS, and of where open source security is headed as a result.

## Benefits to the ecosystem

Open source is at a turning point. For years its security has been a matter of best practice. The Cyber Resilience Act, which entered into force in December 2024 and applies in full from 11 December 2027, turns that into a legal matter, with a named role for the organisations behind open source, dated obligations, and real consequences for the vendors who ship it. The security side of open source is clearly moving from following best practices to complying with the law, and the CRA is only the first of these laws.

Most of the engineers who actually maintain this code have not heard that this is happening. We believe the whole open source world, from maintainers and contributors to the enterprises that fund projects or sell products built on them, needs to know what the CRA is and what it requires of them if their software reaches the EU market.

Our aim with this talk is to give the community that knowledge in one session: which role a project falls into, the exact duties and dates for each role, what downstream vendors will now ask of maintainers, and a short checklist any project can act on. This session also shows how the new duties on vendors can work in the ecosystem's favour, turning vulnerability handling into a shared responsibility between maintainers and the companies that depend on them, instead of a burden carried by volunteers. Everything in it comes from applying the regulation to open source projects we maintain ourselves.
